# Learning from declined lines

The second investigation examined every new retained rejection through event 3537, including the latest older blend refusal. There were 48 new events: 47 tint evaluations and one unfinished draft, representing 38 review records, 42 intermediate rewrite failures and six cuts. Of the tint events, 41 reported missing rhyme, 22 insufficient transformation and eight a semantic mismatch; categories overlap. No new blend failure occurred in this window. The latest older blend proposal dropped much of its conversation, so retaining the original was appropriate. Exact source/candidate pairs and the consistent snapshot identity are in the [fresh audit](adaptive-rejection-audit.json) and [findings](adaptive-rejection-audit.md).

## Acceptance now distinguishes style from meaning

The new **Fluid** mode tolerates two explicit style objections: insufficient lexical transformation and no new vocabulary from the crystal passage. It still requires usable dialogue, positive measured meaning and rap-rhyme evidence, preserved caller structure and the source-copy check. Unknown faults remain blocking. The existing **Strict** mode retains the original configured grading behavior. This mode is separate from the master rejection switch and its blanket allowance; it does not set a general allowance of one fault, which could also waive a missing number or changed meaning.

The raw machine grade remains in each report. A separate `editorial` decision identifies the active mode, remaining blockers, style advisories and exact additional evidence. Preview trials use these same acceptance checks. Changing learning or acceptance revisions invalidates stale trial applies. Restoring Strict also invalidates cached and persisted proof that depended on a Fluid style exception. Existing review votes, saved instructions and original source evidence remain intact.

Two concrete detector corrections make the existing rhyme requirement more accurate. `touch` now has the vowel used by `clutch`, while `touch/couch` is no longer treated as a match. A three-word rhyme landing such as “A disaster bid” can remain visible when it rhymes with an already established full bar. The change preserves ordinary lists, short fillers, final fragments and every original word. The broader first attempt at this segmentation change failed existing list/gallery tests; it was narrowed before deployment.

## The orchestrator can adapt future prompts

`PromptLearningStore` observes durable refusal evidence and changed individual operator decisions. It activates bounded, predefined prompt guidance when a specific failure pattern appears in at least **three different source lines across two known original conversations**. Repeated retries of the same source do not increase that support. Empty/technical output, unsupported gates, old or unknown grades and unidentified parents are excluded and counted visibly. Required rap failures are recognized even when the older spelling-based subcheck reports a pass.

Hints address observed quantities, names, questions, negation, caller structure, rhyme, copied phrases, lost anchors and appended filler. At most four hints apply to one dialogue kind, within a 1,000-character guidance budget. Full source text and user notes cannot turn into executable prompt instructions: the learner selects fixed guidance and retains the exact supporting review IDs and occurrence numbers. Individual approvals retract that source's support for the inferred problem; agreeing with a rejection can confirm it. One-time batch approvals do not train preferences.

Every effective prompt or control change creates an immutable revision with its reason and evidence. Counters alone do not create prompt revisions. Rollback restores a saved revision and pauses adaptation until Resume is chosen, so the next observation cannot immediately undo the rollback. Disabling learning and selecting acceptance mode are independent controls. Manual future instructions remain separate and editable.

Production turn/round prompts and generated workbench trials share the same effective guidance. Captured traces include its text, learning revision and acceptance mode. The discussion view receives active hints and their support, so the orchestrator can explain what changed using recorded evidence. The automatic mechanism is deterministic evidence-based prompt adaptation; it does not invent arbitrary system prompts, run an extra grading model or certify its own writing.

The **Rejected lines → Learn and improve** panel exposes the learning switch, Strict/Fluid selector, active hints with pinned evidence, revision history, Restore/Resume and an explicit refresh of recent decline evidence. Reading or opening it performs no writes. Unsaved controls and drafts survive request failures and reopening; changes use the inspected revision to prevent overwriting newer state.

## Replay and known limits

The [actual grader replay](adaptive-rejection-replay.json) used all 47 tint occurrences, preserved their original forces and grades, and froze 758 vocabulary words from their retained style chunks. It ran in isolated preview with model calls and production review writes blocked. This vocabulary is an approximation to the historical full crystal vocabulary, and these are rejection records rather than a denominator of all generated attempts.

The final replay permits **13 occurrences across seven distinct wording pairs**: three pass the raw grade and ten receive style advisories. All seven pairs were read manually. They retain rough rhyme padding; these are eligibility changes under the user's more permissive style preference, not seven polished rewrites or a station-wide improvement rate. All eight recorded semantic failures still block, and 28 still lack the required rap evidence. See the [manual review and comparison](adaptive-rejection-replay.md).

Manual review also caught three unacceptable admissions in the first Fluid replay: “try to” became an accomplished action, and two unfinished sources acquired orphaned “dense” endings. Narrow evidence-based guards now block those cases while retaining their raw grades. They recognize explicit attempt-to/asserted-action wording and the observed dangling determiner/orphan pattern; they are not a general semantic parser. Other changed propositions or poor phrasing can still escape deterministic screening.

## Validation and deployment evidence

The backend was deployed and learning explicitly enabled in **Fluid mode, revision 2, with 12 qualified reminders**. The master rejection policy remained enabled with zero blanket fault allowance. The station remained on and unpaused. Deployed source hashes and the activation receipt are retained in the [deployment record](prompt-learning-deployment.json).

Final validation passed **787 Python tests in 29.622 seconds, 20 Node tests (13 existing and seven learning checks), and 294 isolated Electron assertions (146 review, 76 workbench and 72 learning)**. These cover actual grading, candidate retention, prompt injection, preview isolation, revision conflicts, rollback, Strict-mode readiness restoration and complete source/word-order preservation. Browser fixtures cover small viewports, unsaved drafts, lost responses and duplicate clicks; the learning fixture also runs the exact delivered-page inspector against real DOM. See the [validation manifest](prompt-learning-validation.json), [learning fixture](prompt-learning-ui-check.json), [active reminder capture](prompt-learning-active-hints.png) and [mobile capture](prompt-learning-mobile.png).

The authenticated [live UI inspection](prompt-learning-live-ui-check.json) opened both Radio and Control, verified the saved mode, revision and all 12 reminders, and read revision history. No requests that write state were sent and no media played. The test guard did block three background POST attempts from the surrounding pages, prevent 14 playback attempts and block 10 media requests; the zero-write result does not mean those pages made no background attempts. There were no review-module errors. Actual delivered controls and evidence are visible in the [live screenshot](prompt-learning-live-active.png) and [Radio history](prompt-learning-live-radio-learning-history.png).

Two authorized isolated [wording trials](prompt-learning-rewrite-trials.md) then used the current shared prompt with thinking off and no extra operator instruction. Both passed the raw machine checks and were accepted without a style waiver. Manual reading still found unclear meaning and filler: the banter lost explicit past tense and introduced "hit that fact"; the news retained its core instruction but added "the vision's near." Three learned reminders were present verbatim in the banter request. No news-specific reminders qualified, so the news request contained only the shared Fluid guidance. Exact sources, outputs, traces and timings are in the [trial evidence](prompt-learning-rewrite-trials.json). These samples verify the generation path and expose its limits; they do not establish that learning caused better wording.

The separate [production observation](prompt-learning-production.json) used three stock snapshots spanning 187.643 seconds and actual model and media receipts after activation, excluding both diagnostic traces. Twelve production requests reached the model: six carried revision 2 reminders, while six belonged to a round that started before activation and retained its captured context. Eleven calls completed, with observed queue waits of 7.349-26.335 seconds and wire durations of 9.096-24.796 seconds. Exact normalized source matching covered 195 row occurrences across 36 original source hashes.

That short window contained two raw/effective passing grade occurrences, 21 refusals and no production style-waiver pass. Those counts include repeated progress snapshots and are not a distinct-attempt acceptance rate. One fresh uncached XTTS track-talk receipt produced 18.1 seconds of audio. Ready stock moved from 36 to 35 while the station was playing; one accepted item still awaited recording and the sampled recording booths were idle. There were no additional automatic hint revisions or learning errors. This establishes that production received the reminders and continued doing work, while leaving acceptance-rate improvement and complete recording-room throughput unproven.

The update enables future prompt learning and the selected acceptance mode. It does not bulk-vote on historical lines, install benchmark wording or start playback. Existing active work can use the revised checks through normal writing and recording. A continuously problem-free station is not established by a bounded replay or short live sample; prompt revisions and remaining faults stay inspectable for subsequent refinement.
